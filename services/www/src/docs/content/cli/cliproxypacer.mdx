A chat on the [CLIProxyPacer provider](/providers#cliproxypacer) gets four extras in the TUI: a cache item in the
toolbar, an **Account** section in the sidebar, a **Requests** tab in the footer, and two switches. Without CPP set up,
none of the extras shows.

- The **Requests** tab shows for a chat that used CLIProxyPacer (CPP): its model is one of CPP's, or one of its steps
  went through CPP.
- The cache item, the **Account** section and the switches show only while the chat's model is one of CPP's: the model
  selected for it, else the one its last reply came from. A chat moved to another provider keeps its **Requests** tab.

```text
warm 23:41
```

The keys are listed on [Keybinds](/cli/keybinds#cliproxypacer).

## Toolbar

The toolbar under the prompt shows one cache item after the context and cost. It counts down to when the chat's prompt
cache goes cold if nothing more is sent, including the pings CPP still plans.

| It shows                                         | When                                                                                                 |
| ------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `warm 23:41`                                     | the cache is warm and CPP pings it                                                                   |
| `warming off · warm 3:12`                        | warming is off for the chat; the cache is still warm                                                 |
| `cache cold`                                     | the cache went cold, shown as a warning                                                              |
| `keep-alive stopped · warm 2:10`                 | CPP's pings stopped on a failure, shown as a warning                                                 |
| `compacts at 14:32`                              | compact before cold is on, and CPP has set the time it asks for it                                   |
| `account used up · next step moves to <account>` | the chat's account can't serve its next step, shown as a warning                                     |
| `warm`                                           | the cache is warm and CPP gives no time                                                              |
| nothing                                          | the proxy has no pacer, CPP knows nothing of the chat yet, or the chat's model is another provider's |

- While CPP is unreachable the item keeps its last text, greyed, with the time it is from: `warm 12:05 · as of 14:02`.
  It stops counting until CPP answers again.
- In a narrow window the shortcuts go first, then the context and cost; the cache item goes last.
- Click it to open the footer at the **Requests** tab.

## Sidebar

The **Account** section, above the sidebar's other sections, shows the account CPP serves the chat from, or the one that
served it last while the chat is idle. The sidebar's **Context** section is gone; the toolbar shows the same.

```text
Account
claude-max-2
5h  62% left · resets 1:20
7d  88% left · resets ~3d 4h
next: claude-max-1, once its cache is cold
```

- Each line under the account is one of its usage windows. `~` marks a reset CPP estimates, `· stale` a reading CPP no
  longer counts on (older than an hour), `not started` a window nothing has used since it last reset, and
  `reset, awaiting a reading` one whose reset has passed with no reading since.
- `next:` says where the chat goes once its cache is cold, and why.
- `forced move to <account> now` (a warning) or `… soon` says CPP must move the chat.
- While CPP is unreachable, the section is greyed and ends with `as of 14:02`. On a proxy without the pacer it says
  `no pacer`.

## Requests tab

The **Requests** tab lists the chat's requests at CPP, with its subagents', newest at the top. Press **↓** from the
prompt to open the footer, then **←/→** to reach the tab after **Terminals**, or click the toolbar's cache item.

```text
time      kind        model               account       read/write    verdict                             cost
14:02:13  step        claude-sonnet-5-5   claude-max-2  48.2K/1.0K    hit                              $0.0312
14:01:40  explore     claude-haiku-5-5    claude-max-2  0/12.5K       miss · tools changed · tools[0]  $0.0451
14:00:02  title       claude-haiku-5-5    claude-max-2  0/0                                            $0.0004
```

- **kind** is `step`, the agent of a subagent's step, `ping`, `title`, `compaction` or `search`, a call of one of CPP's
  [web tools](#web-tools): its model is the tool, its account the service's key.
- **read/write** are the tokens read from and written to the prompt cache.
- **verdict** is CPP's cache verdict. A miss shows its cause and the part of the prompt that changed, as a warning; a
  failed or refused request shows as an error.
- The columns are as wide as their values need, so a value is cut only when the line has no room left. They keep their
  widths while the tab stays open; a longer value that arrives meanwhile is cut until you open the tab again or resize
  the window.
- In a narrow window the model goes first, then the account, then read/write.
- With the newest row selected, a new request takes the selection, so the newest stays under the cursor. With any other
  row selected, the selection stays on its request as new rows arrive at the top.
- More rows are read as you near the end of the list.

| Key                             | Does                                                                                                                                                                          |
| ------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **↑/↓**, **Page Up/Down**       | Move between requests; **↑** on the newest closes the footer.                                                                                                                 |
| **Ctrl+A**                      | Shows only cache misses, or all requests again. The TUI remembers it until it exits, as it does the Subagents filter.                                                         |
| **Enter**                       | Moves the chat to where the request ended: the end of that step's reply. A subagent's request opens the subagent's chat there; a ping or a title goes to the reply before it. |
| **Ctrl+Enter** or **Alt+Enter** | Shows what changed in the request's prompt, in place of the list. The same key or **Esc** goes back to the same row.                                                          |

What changed shows CPP's verdict and its cause, how many tokens were written to the cache again and what the miss cost,
and the request it was compared with. Below that come the parts of the prompt that changed, are new, or only had their
cache marker moved. It scrolls with **↑/↓**, and the footer grows to at most half the window. When CPP no longer keeps
the prompt, it says `prompt no longer kept`.

```text
miss · tools changed · tools[0] changed
12.5K tokens written again · the miss cost $0.0389
compared with 1793911200-claude-max-2-7f3a of 14:00:02
changed  tool       tools[0]  1.2K chars  read: Read a file from the local filesystem
14 unchanged
```

**Inside tmux use Alt+Enter.** tmux passes a modified Enter on only with its `extended-keys` option; without it,
Ctrl+Enter can arrive as a plain Enter, which moves the chat to the request instead. Alt+Enter gets through. In macOS
Terminal it needs **Use Option as Meta key** in the profile's keyboard settings.

The tab says why it is empty: `No requests yet`, `No cache misses yet`, `CPP unreachable · as of 14:02` above the last
rows, greyed, or `This proxy has no pacer, so it keeps no request records`. It is hidden when CPP isn't set up or the
chat never used it.

## Switches

Two switches change how CPP treats the chat. The leader is **Ctrl+X** unless you changed it, so `<leader>k` is
**Ctrl+X**, then **K**. Each is also in the command palette (**Ctrl+P**), under CLIProxyPacer.

| Key         | Palette                           | Switches                                                                    |
| ----------- | --------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `<leader>k` | Cache warming for this chat       | CPP's pings for the chat, which keep its prompt cache warm while you pause. |
| `<leader>j` | Compact before cold for this chat | Whether CPP asks to compact the idle chat before its cache goes cold.       |

```text
Cache warming off for this chat
```

- A toast says the new state, and the toolbar shows it at once.
- A switch changed elsewhere, such as on CPP's own page, shows the next time the TUI shows the chat.
- Before the chat's first reply, CPP knows nothing of it yet: switch it after that reply.
- On a proxy without the pacer, or for a chat whose model is another provider's, neither the keys nor the palette
  entries exist.
- When CPP asks to compact, openforge compacts the chat with its own model on CPP, the one whose prompt cache is warm,
  never a compaction model set elsewhere. It leaves the chat alone while a step runs, a prompt is queued or a revert is
  staged, and when its model is no longer on CPP.

**Warm while waiting** needs no switch. While a chat waits on a command in the foreground or on a subagent, openforge
asks CPP to keep pinging it for up to 4 hours, and stops when the wait ends. Commands in the background don't count.

## Web tools

When CPP has web tools, every chat gets them, also a chat on another provider's model: `web_search`, `web_research`,
`web_extract` and `web_crawl` (see [Web tools](/providers#web-tools)). They show in the chat as tool calls.

- A call asks first, as the built-in web search and `webfetch` do (see [Approvals](/permissions#approvals)).
- A call CPP can't serve shows as a failed tool call, with CPP's reason, which the model reads too.
- A research or a crawl that outlasts one call shows the job's ID; the model's next call of the tool, with that ID, gets
  the result.
